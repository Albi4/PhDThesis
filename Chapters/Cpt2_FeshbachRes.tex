\section{Feshabch resonances in ultracold gases}


Magnetically tunable Feshbach resonances are one of the central tools of ultracold-atom experiments. As discussed in the previous chapters, they allow the interaction strength between two atoms to be controlled by changing an external magnetic field. At the two-body level, this tunability originates from the coupling between an open scattering channel and a molecular bound state in a closed channel. When the energy of this bound state is tuned close to the scattering threshold, the scattering length becomes resonantly enhanced and can be varied over a large range. On one side of the resonance, the two atoms support a weakly bound molecular state, commonly referred to as a Feshbach molecule.
