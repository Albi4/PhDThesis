\chapter{Additional measurements on the D\lowercase{y}-K Feshbach spectrum}
\label{Cpt:AddDyKRes}

This chapter is dedicated to the study of Feshbach resonances outside the low-field region that has been the primary focus of our recent efforts. This work has been done in parallel with the studies reported in Chapter \ref{Chpt:Lowfieldpaper} and \ref{Chpt:MolTrap}, and thus with the crucial contributions of Zhu-Xiong Ye and Elisa Soave. In Section \ref{Sec:Compendium}, we explore the interaction spectrum over a large range of magnetic fields and, in Section \ref{Sec:RF217}, we present an attempt to characterize the broad resonance at \SI{217}{G}.



\section{Compendium of  D\lowercase{y}-K Feshbach resonance}
\label{Sec:Compendium}

All of the main results presented in this thesis are obtained working around the interspecies Feshbach resonance close to \SI{7}{G}. While this resonance proved to be a very fruitful tool for studying interspecies interactions and Feshbach molecules, we also invested some time to re-explore the interaction spectrum in the mixture at different magnetic fields, with the objective, and hope, to find other and possibly broader candidates. A similar study was already performed by  previous member of the team, when looking for a strong and broad resonance, which resulted in the identification of the resonance at \SI{217}{G} reported in Ref.~\cite{Ravensbergen2020rif}. However in the years following that study, considerable effort was made to improve the control and stability of the magnetic field in the experiment\footnote{see also Appendix \ref{app:MagneticField} for a presentation of some of the improvements}, which motivated us to re-explore the entire magnetic field range accessible with our experimental setup.

The approach we employed to identify interspecies resonances is the same as the one presented in Sec.~\ref{sec:InterThemScan}. After evaporative cooling of the mixture, always performed at low magnetic fields, we parametrically heat the potassium atomic cloud and then ramp the magnetic field to a target value, at which the two clouds are allowed to thermalize for a fixed amount of time. Strong interspecies interaction corresponds to peaks in the thermalization rate, which we associate with a Feshbach resonance. The magnetic field resolution is of \SI{20}{mG} for fields below \SI{70}{G}, and of \SI{200}{mG} above that. This choice reflects the fact that below \SI{70}{G}, we were looking for \textit{any} resonance, while above that we were focusing only on identifying \textit{broad} resonances, which would justify the additional troubles of working at high magnetic fields. The coarse scan at higher magnetic fields probably missed different narrower features.

The results of this Feshbach spectroscopy are reported in Figures \ref{fig:3_19G_Scan} through \ref{fig:260_360G_Scan}. We identified 35 interspecies Feshbach resonances investigating magnetic fields up to \SI{360}G, which is close to the upper limit of our coils. Ultimately, no suitable broader resonance was found. No other feature broader than the one at \SI{217}{G} was observed, except for a possible candidate around \SI{340}{G} which appeared to have a somewhat comparable strength. However, we did not characterize this resonance any further as we deemed it to be at too high magnetic field, and  both the large number of resonances that would need to be crossed to reach it and the high magnetic field would not allow us to prepare and control efficiently the quantum mixture. All  other resonances observed are of comparable width to, or narrower than, the \SI{7}{G} resonance, but without the advantage of being at low magnetic field. Table \ref{tab:AllFeshbach} summarizes our findings.

\section{Binding energy measurements at the $217$\,G Resonance}
\label{Sec:RF217}

Since the resonance located near \SI{217}{G} is the broadest and strongest resonance identified in the Dy-K mixture, we attempted to characterize it with a  similar approach to the one employed for the low-magnetic-field resonances, i.e. by measuring the binding energy of the molecular state associated with the Feshbach resonance.  The experimental approach used closely resembles the one presented in Sec.~\ref{sec:BEM}, albeit at higher magnetic fields. Our motivation here was to apply the techniques developed in Chapter \ref{Chpt:Lowfieldpaper} to a broader resonance and, once the resonance was thoroughly  characterized, use the resonance as a gateway to achieving superfluidity.

\begin{figure}[thb]
	\includegraphics[trim=0cm 0 0cm 0, clip, width=1\columnwidth]{gfx/Cpt6_fig/Binding_energy_217G.pdf}
	\caption{Binding energy of Feshbach molecules around the magnetic field region of the $217$ G resonance. In red the data point fitted according to eq.\ref{equ:bindingEnergyCurve}  \label{fig:RF_spectra_217G}}
\end{figure}

The results of the magnetic field modulation are reported in Fig.~\ref{fig:RF_spectra_217G}. The interpretation of the results of this measurement is not straightforward. In two separate region of magnetic field, indicated by the shaded areas in the figure,  the wiggle spectroscopy did not return any meaningful loss feature to associate  with the  molecular spectrum. We attribute this to the presence of narrow Feshbach resonances located at these fields, that significantly complicates  the molecular spectrum underlying the resonances. The presence of additional resonances, not observed in the thermalization spectroscopy, is further corroborated by the behavior of the measured binding energy. In the vicinity of the shaded areas the binding energy values measured deviate from the behavior expected near a broad Feshbach resonance. This discrepancy can be interpreted in terms of avoided crossing between the molecular states underlying the different resonances. 

As a guide for the eye, we plotted different possible molecular states in Fig.~\ref{fig:RF_spectra_217G}. The green dashed line is the binding energy that would be associated to an isolated broad Feshbach resonance calculated according to Eq.~\ref{equ:bindingEnergyCurve}  using the parameters (i.e. differential magnetic moment,  R$^*$ and $A$)  estimated in Ref.~\cite{Ravensbergen2020rif}. The red solid line instead is a fit, using the same free parameters as those used in Sec.~\ref{sec:BEM}, of Eq.~\ref{equ:bindingEnergyCurve} to the data points at magnetic fields $> \SI{215.9}{G}$, i.e. for the data obtained at magnetic fields above the larger of the two overlapping narrow resonances. The dashed blue and orange lines are fit relative to a smaller portion of the magnetic field in the vicinity of the shaded areas and suggest the possible behavior of the molecular states of the narrow resonances.


In conclusion, we partially characterized the \SI{217}{G} resonance, by measuring the molecular bound state energy. However, this measurement showed that the broad resonance is not well isolated and the presence of narrow resonances prevented a clean characterization. The presence of these narrow resonances also complicates the precise tuning of interactions and he prospect of reaching the universal regime at the broad resonance. Attempts to perform magneto association of Feshbach molecules at the broad resonance, with a similar approach to what is presented in Chapter \ref{Chpt:MolTrap}, were unsuccessful, as the procedure to follow adiabatically the bound state  were disrupted by the narrow resonances.

In this chapter, we have presented two sets of measurements in which we attempted to identify and characterize possible alternatives to the \SI{7}{G} resonance. With a thermalization scan we identify a rich interspecies Feshbach spectrum, without however identifying any candidates broader than the previously observed resonances. We have characterized the molecular spectrum of the \SI{217}{G} resonance, identifying the presence of two narrows feature in the vicinity of the pole of the broad resonance. These findings motivated us to stick with the \SI{7}{G} resonance as the most suitable platform for studying the Dy-K mixture in the strongly interacting regime. In the next part of this thesis, we will present our efforts to study and characterize DyK Feshbach molecules associated across the \SI{7}{G} resonance.
