\chapter{What have we learned?}
\label{Chpt:Summary}

In this chapter we summarize the main lessons learned from the Dy-K experiment, from both a technical and a conceptual perspective. In the next chapter, we instead will provide a broader outlook on future experiments concerned in the study of mass-imbalance Fermi gases and more broadly to unconventional quantum mixtures.

The first important lesson drawn from the experiment, which is probably not (yet) fully conveyed in the publications from our group, is the difficulty of producing the doubly degenerate mixture. While evaporative cooling of dysprosium and the simultaneous sympathetic cooling of potassium are reliable and work very well, satisfactory cooling is achieved only at very low magnetic fields, below \SI{500}{mG}. Above this magnetic field, the interaction spectrum of Dy is found to be strongly temperature-dependent \cite{Krstajic2025cot}, and severe three-body losses prevent efficient evaporative cooling. These limitations, for a long time neglected in order to study the mixture, are currently being explored in our experiment.
