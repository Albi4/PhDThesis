\chapter{Thesis Outline}


In the rest of the thesis, we present our work on the Dy-K system.  This work is organized in three additional Parts.

Part \hyperref[pt:questDyKFR]{II} is dedicated to the characterization of the interspecies interaction spectrum of the Dy-K mixture.
\begin{itemize}
	\item In Chapter \ref{Chpt:expIntro},  we introduce to the Dy-K experiment,  detailing the experimental sequence routinely employed in the experiment to produce a doubly degenerate quantum mixture. We further contextualize the experimental findings of this thesis by briefly summarizing the main scientific achievement obtained in the past.
	
	\item Chapter \ref{Chpt:Lowfieldpaper} is dedicated to the results reported in Ref.~\cite{Ye2022ool}. In this publication we explore the low magnetic field region, below \SI{10}{G}, of the Dy-K interaction spectrum, identifying and characterizing $5$ interspecies Feshbach resonances. A particularly well isolated Feshbach resonance close to \SI{7}{G}, exhibiting a meaningful universal regime, is identified as a suitable candidate for interaction tuning.
	
	\item  In Chapter \ref{Chpt:DipoleandHydro}, also reported in Ref.~\cite{Ye2025dms}, we precisely characterize interactions at the \SI{7}{G} resonance, by observing the collisional hydrodynamic crossover through the study of dipole-mode oscillations at different interaction strengths. From our experimental results, we are able to reconstruct the full spectrum associated with the dipole-mode oscillation, additionally identifying contribution from mean field interactions. By the study of a purely dissipative modes, occurring at time scales faster than any other relevant phenomena, we also characterize the interspecies drag effect.   
	
	\item In  Chapter \ref{Chpt:AddDyKRes}, we close this part of the thesis, reporting additional measurements on the Dy-K interaction spectrum. We performed a thermalization measurement between dysprosium and potassium in a magnetic field range up to \SI{360}{G}, identifying $42$ Feshbach resonances. We additionally characterize the broadest Feshbach resonance identified, by measuring the molecular bound state underlying the Feshbach resonance, and we  the presence of additional narrow resonance located near the broad resonance pole.
	
\end{itemize}
