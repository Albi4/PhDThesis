\chapter{Thesis Outline}
\label{Chpt:Outline}

The rest of this thesis details our efforts towards characterizing interactions between dysprosium and potassium and their Feshbach molecules.  The thesis is therefore divided in two large topics.

Part \hyperref[pt:questDyKFR]{II} is dedicated to the characterization of the interspecies interaction spectrum of the Dy-K mixture, with a focus on the low magnetic field region, where we identified a particularly promising Feshbach resonance.
\begin{itemize}
	\item In Chapter \ref{Chpt:expIntro},  we introduce the Dy-K experiment,  detailing the experimental sequence routinely employed in the experiment to produce a doubly degenerate quantum mixture. We further contextualize the experimental findings of this thesis by briefly summarizing the main scientific achievement obtained in the past.
	
	\item Chapter \ref{Chpt:Lowfieldpaper} is dedicated to the results reported in Ref.~\cite{Ye2022ool}. In this publication we explore the low magnetic field region, below \SI{10}{G}, of the Dy-K interaction spectrum, identifying and characterizing $5$ interspecies Feshbach resonances. A particularly well-isolated Feshbach resonance close to \SI{7}{G}, exhibiting a meaningful universal regime, is identified as a suitable candidate for interaction tuning.
	
	\item  In Chapter \ref{Chpt:DipoleandHydro}, also reported in Ref.~\cite{Ye2025dms}, we precisely characterize interactions at the \SI{7}{G} resonance, by observing the collisional hydrodynamic crossover through the study of dipole-mode oscillations at different interaction strengths. From our experimental results, we are able to reconstruct the full spectrum associated with the dipole-mode oscillations, additionally identifying contributions from mean field interactions. Through the study of a purely dissipative mode, occurring on timescales shorter than any other relevant dynamical scale, we also characterize the interspecies drag effect.   
	
	\item In  Chapter \ref{Chpt:AddDyKRes}, we conclude this part of the thesis by reporting additional measurements on the Dy-K interaction spectrum. We performed a thermalization measurement between dysprosium and potassium in a magnetic field range up to \SI{360}{G}, identifying $42$ Feshbach resonances. We additionally characterize the broadest Feshbach resonance identified, by measuring the molecular bound state underlying the Feshbach resonance, and we identify  the presence of additional narrow resonances located near the broad resonance pole.
	
\end{itemize}

Part \hyperref[pt:MakingProbing]{III} is dedicated to the study and characterization of DyK Feshbach molecules, associated across the \SI{7}{G} resonance.

\begin{itemize}
	\item In Chapter \ref{Chpt:FeshmolIntro}, we provide some additional context concerning Feshbach molecules and their relevance to the \ac{BEC}-\ac{BCS} crossover, providing a brief historical overview and presenting some connections to the broader molecular physics field..
	
	\item Chapter \ref{Chpt:MolTrap}, published as Ref.~\cite{Soave2023otf}, details our progress toward the observation and controlled manipulation of DyK molecules. We present a reliable protocol to associate and prepare an optically-trapped pure sample of molecules. We precisely characterize the resonance parameters through two independent measurements of the molecular bound state properties. Additionally, we measure the phase-space density of the molecules and identify losses induced by the trapping light as the dominant decay mechanism of the molecular sample.
	
	
	\item In Chapter \ref{Chpt:MolLoss}, published as Ref.~\cite{Canali2026otf}, we build upon the results of the previous chapter, by characterizing the dominant loss mechanisms in the molecular sample. We perform spectroscopy around two different regions of the infrared spectrum and we characterize light-induced losses at four different wavelengths, demonstrating that longer wavelengths significantly extend the molecular lifetime. Finally, by using a trap with reduced light losses we observed inelastic collisional effects contributing to the decay of the molecular sample. For very-weakly bound dimers, we demonstrate Pauli suppression of collisional losses. 
	
\end{itemize}



In Part \hyperref[pt:End]{IV}, we conclude this thesis. First, in Chapter \ref{Chpt:Summary} we summarize the results and the lessons learned from the Dy-K experiment, both from a scientific and a technical perspective. Subsequently in Chapter \ref{Chpt:Outlook}, we give a broader outlook, focusing on future directions explorable with a Dy-K mixture and also draw connections to other quantum-mixtures experiments that are being developed.
